# Sample Content - borrowed voices for style learning

4 LinkedIn posts, from 4 different creators. These are samples the user asked
to learn the writing style from (Option B). They are NOT the user's own writing;
they show the cadence/hook/flow style to borrow for feel. The hard-bans and the
six interview stories remain the source of truth for substance.

## Sample 1 - Dean D. (marketing, brand-strategy voice)
Hook: "The hardest marketing briefs aren't tight deadlines or tiny budgets.
It's a product when no one knows why."
Pattern: long-form reflective essay in short lines. Builds a frustration, then
delivers a numbered framework (6 origins of innovation). Ends with a direct,
mild command ("Ask the question early. Why does this exist?").
Style markers: rhetorical questions, "So you..." second-person thread, list
framework, aphoristic close ("you can't build a bridge when nobody told you
where it needs to go").

## Sample 2 - Charlie Hills (AI/tools, automation voice)
Hook: "Your AI automations break when you're not looking."
Pattern: short punchy lines, numbered how-to (6 steps), then a one-line kicker
("So nothing runs until you've checked it"). Casual humor, emoji-light, direct
value, repost CTA. Very short bursts between steps.
Style markers: imperative step list, plain-language tool talk, "the irony
writes itself" one-liners, conversational.

## Sample 3 - Connor Gillivan (SaaS founder, personal-story voice)
Hook: "Most weeks I've got north of 20 calls on the calendar."
Pattern: first-person founder story with a real number up front. Builds a
specific pain (forgetting across 6 businesses), then the one small change
(a Granola pre-call brief). Ends with the results + a soft P.S. CTA.
Style markers: specific numbers, "the thing I'd otherwise be digging for",
modest self-deprecation ("nod along, then go hunt for the note"), concrete
outcome, P.S. hook for the persona.

## Sample 4 - Axelle Malek (AI productivity, how-to voice)
Hook: "Typing every prompt is the slow way to use AI."
Pattern: contrarian claim, then "I stopped typing mine and started talking."
Numbered reasons, then a setup/how-to list (→ arrows), then a "my favorite
part" beat. Ends on a clean strong line ("That pretty useless Fn key finally
has a job").
Style markers: short contrarian hook, arrow-prefixed setup steps, concrete
personal adoption, a witty closing observation.
