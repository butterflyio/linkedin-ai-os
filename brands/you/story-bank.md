# Story Bank - Zain Khan

The six real stories from the 29 Oct 2026 voice interview. These are the ONLY
proven sources to pull from when drafting. Never invent a story or add a
detail that is not here - ask if a fact is missing.

## Story 1 - The win: the loan that made me move

At 18 I took a student loan so my parents would not pay for my education. The
pressure to pay it back made me set a rule: figure out how to work for myself
before the job gets comfortable. I didn't know the word entrepreneurship. It
was near impossible to find co-working spaces in Delhi back then. I chased an
olive-cultivation internship in Rajasthan (Olive Cultivation Limited, ROCL) -
rode a Jeep across small towns, worked with a consultant, turned it into my
final-year report. College ended, I took a job at Wipro in Bangalore. Gave
myself three months. Got out by cold-emailing importers offering market
research in the olive-oil and HoReCa space. Two said yes, about 500 EUR a
month, a fortune to me then. Called my parents to Bangalore, showed the offer
and payment letters, they said go. That became OLIVA International, then the
Zeytin brand. Later: 40 days backpacking Italy, met producers, became the
first olive-oil taster from India. Sold the business when conglomerates
squeezed the margins.

## Story 2 - The failure: a second brand too early

The first brand was working - mass market, distributors buying, imports from
Italy, Spain, Portugal. I thought: why not a premium niche second brand? The
idea was not wrong; that is how markets work. The mistake was timing. I
launched too early - before the first brand saturated, before new markets,
before partners who could carry it. I priced it high and told myself the
market would catch up. It didn't. We lost money. Lesson: some decisions in
business are urgent; this one was not. There is a beauty in waiting - waiting
matures the thing itself. A good idea launched too early is just a bill.

## Story 3 - The pivot: sold it, then learned how jobs work

After selling and spending about a year in Bangalore working beside the buyer,
I came home to Delhi and did something that confused people: I took a real
job. I'd never really worked as an employee. I wanted to understand how the
job market works before touching another venture. Joined Tripoto, a travel
startup. Learned soft skills entrepreneurship had let me skip - communicate,
sell, present, sit in a room with peers. Made lifelong friends. Then COVID
killed travel overnight. Jobless at home, I reflected. Pivoted to teaching
English - it was native to me (English-medium school), but I got certified
anyway: TEFL (The TEFL Academy), then CELPIP. Built a community, mentored 500+
professionals toward Canada, better jobs, new countries. Hit the real ceiling:
this business ran on my time. One person, one hour at a time.

## Story 4 - The country shift: alone in a new land

The hardest reset was not a business decision - it was moving countries alone.
Back home I had everything: car, house, parents, friends, a whole network.
Then I moved Delhi to Dubai on a three-month tourist visa, no sponsorship, no
contacts, little money. Where to stay, how to commute, what to eat, how to
find work - everything that was handled for me became my problem. Lived off
Sheikh Zayed Road, co-worked near Emirates Towers, attended almost every free
event on Luma, gave interviews. By the last month I was close to giving up.
One small customer-service role opened up, leaning on my English skills -
nothing you build ever goes to waste. It bought breathing room and a visa.
Then I found a role in Web3 (no regulator existed yet; the government was
drawing builders in). Ran two jobs, quit the traditional one, went full-time
Web3. Leading the UAE region in crypto marketing came next.

## Story 5 - The build now: Promptraise and BuzzzAgentic

Working in Web3 and marketing, I was always scared of the terminal - alien,
out of comfort zone. I'd had a GitHub for 7-8 years with almost no commits. In
2025 I gave myself a goal: learn the terminal, use AI efficiently and safely.
It opened VPSs and agentic frameworks, and I could actually run, break, and
test them. Then I saw the real problem: it is easy to build a website, but
hard to get traffic. That led to Promptraise - Web3 visibility and traffic
through SEO, GEO, AEO. I partnered with Max Moris (cicada) to build it. Around
the same time I formed BuzzzAgentic - an agentic AI operating system for
social media that lets businesses run their presence from WhatsApp or
Telegram. ~678 GitHub contributions in 2026. AI was a big accelerator; but
while using these systems I felt the vulnerabilities, and understood it is not
safe enough yet for normal people.

## Story 6 - The plunge: AI safety and effective altruism

The same instinct that made me take the harder path every time - the loan, the
olive migration, teaching 500 people, landing alone in Dubai - is behind what
I'm building now. I went deeper into AI safety: BlueDot courses, LessWrong,
80,000 Hours, exploring fellowships and research where I can contribute as a
generalist. This is effective altruism - giving back in the most evidence-
based way, not as a gesture. AI alignment is a part of AI safety. Whatever I
am and wherever I end up happened because of the magic of people; no matter how
much technology matures, human connection never loses its flavour. Two
businesses, and an active contribution to the space of AI safety.
